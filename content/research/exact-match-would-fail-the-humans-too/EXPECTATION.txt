Written 2026-11-15, before running anything on the data.

Setup: SQuAD v1.1 dev (10,570 questions, mostly 3 human answers each). For every
question, each human answer in turn is the "prediction" and the other human answers
are the references (max over references, as the official script does). Three
metrics: exact match (official normalisation), token F1, and cosine similarity of
bge-small-en-v1.5 embeddings of the two answer strings. Then ~100 adversarial wrong
answers I write by hand, and a hand-read sample of 50 exact-match failures.

What I expect:
1. Exact match marks about 20% of correct human answers wrong (the SQuAD paper
   reports 80.3% EM for humans on dev, so I expect 17 to 23% failures here).
2. Token F1 at a 0.5 threshold fails about 7% of them; at 0.8 about 13%.
3. Embedding cosine at 0.80 fails under 3%; at 0.85 about 5%; at 0.90 about 12%.
   (The model card says scores live mostly in 0.6 to 1, so thresholds of 0.8 to 0.9
   are where I should look.)
4. On my adversarial set, exact match lets through 0%. F1 >= 0.5 lets through about
   40% (wrong answers that share most of their tokens with the right one). Cosine
   >= 0.85 lets through more than 70%, and cosine >= 0.90 still more than 40%.
5. No single cosine threshold gets both under 10%: the human-failure curve and the
   adversarial-pass curve cross well above that.
6. Of 50 hand-read exact-match failures, most (60% or more) are extra or missing
   words or formatting; under 15% are genuinely different answers.
7. Control: pairing each question's answer with a random other question's answer
   (obviously wrong), cosine >= 0.80 lets through about 10%; F1 and EM about 0%.

What would surprise me: cosine at 0.9 failing fewer than 5% of humans while passing
fewer than 30% of the adversarial set (a clean threshold), or exact match failing
fewer than 10% of human answers.
