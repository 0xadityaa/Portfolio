We live in a culture that demands immediate proof of concept. If you cannot show the metrics, the revenue, or the applause, the world assumes you are failing.

Vincent van Gogh completely rejected this idea.

In January 1885, he was working in obscurity in the village of Nuenen, financially dependent on his brother Theo. Yet he wrote Theo a letter containing a profound psychological anchor ([letter 480](https://vangoghletters.org/en/let480), Nuenen, on or about Monday, 26 January 1885):

> “…if I’m no good now, I won’t be any good later either — but if later, then now too. For wheat is wheat, even if it looks like grass at first to townsfolk — and the other way round too.”

Van Gogh was tapping into an ancient framework for resilience. Here is the three-part philosophy behind his mindset:

## 1. Know the Difference Between Value and Validation

We constantly confuse instrumental value (what the market currently rewards) with intrinsic value (what a thing actually is by its nature). When an untrained eye looks at a young sprout and calls it a weed, that is a failure of perception in the observer. It is never a flaw in the seed.

## 2. Master the Dichotomy of Control

Though he was not a practicing Stoic, Van Gogh lands where Epictetus begins his [Enchiridion](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html): firmly separate what is up to you from what is not. Your own actions are up to you. Your reputation is not. You own the discipline, the hours, and the brushstrokes. The world owns the recognition. Handing your self-worth over to people who cannot tell wheat from grass is a recipe for misery.

## 3. Live According to Your Nature

The Roman Emperor Marcus Aurelius turned to the natural world to explain doing good work. In [the Meditations (5.6)](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.5.five.html) he points to a vine that bears grapes and seeks nothing more once it has produced its proper fruit. A person who has done something good, he adds, does not call out for others to come and see.

If you are writing, coding, painting, or building something in total silence right now, you are not failing. You are just in the grass phase.

Keep your roots down. The harvest does not create the wheat. It only reveals it. 🌾
